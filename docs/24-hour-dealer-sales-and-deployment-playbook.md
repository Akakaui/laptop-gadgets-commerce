# 24-hour Nigerian laptop-dealer sales and deployment playbook

## Purpose

This playbook is designed for selling and launching one independent copy of the ecommerce template for one Nigerian laptop dealer within 24 hours. It assumes the dealer owns or can provide a domain, has a payment-provider account or is ready to create one, and can supply a starter catalog and business information quickly.

The product is sold as a **branded online shop plus private admin dashboard**, not as a multi-tenant SaaS. Every dealer receives a separate copied project, Supabase project, deployment, domain, credentials, and catalog.

> **Core promise:** “I will give you a branded online store where customers can browse and order, plus an admin dashboard where you control products, prices, stock, orders, coupons, delivery, reviews, email leads, and sales information from anywhere.”

## 1. Offer and positioning

Do not sell it as “just a website.” A laptop dealer can already post products on WhatsApp or Instagram. Sell the missing business system: a professional catalog that works continuously, captures customer details, collects orders, supports payment and delivery, and gives the owner control after launch.

The best entry offer is a **24-hour dealer launch package**. The package includes branding, a live storefront, catalog setup, admin access, checkout configuration, basic delivery settings, deployment, testing, and a short owner handover. Domain, hosting, payment-provider fees, logistics fees, photography, bulk catalog entry, marketing, and ongoing support should be listed separately.

| Package | Suggested price | Include | Exclude or charge separately |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Starter launch | ₦50,000 | Branding, up to 15 products, storefront, cart, checkout, basic delivery settings, admin access, deployment guidance, 30-minute handover | Domain, hosting, product photography, bulk catalog import, paid integrations, ongoing maintenance |
| Professional launch | ₦100,000 | Custom UI treatment, up to 50 products, categories, stock controls, coupons, reviews, email collection, analytics, payment/shipping setup support, policy pages, deployment, owner training | Domain, hosting, payment/logistics fees, professional photography, marketing campaigns, monthly support |
| Add-ons | Quote separately | Bulk catalog import, live logistics automation, Mailchimp/Resend setup, WhatsApp notifications, staff accounts, SEO pages, custom reports, maintenance | Any feature requiring a separate paid provider or substantial custom development |

Do not promise unlimited custom development inside ₦50,000–₦100,000. Promise a reusable ecommerce engine, a focused brand redesign, and a defined catalog/setup scope. This protects your margin and makes the offer repeatable.

## 2. Who to approach

Prioritize dealers who already have stock and customer demand but are losing time or credibility because their online sales process is manual. Good prospects include laptop shops with active WhatsApp or Instagram pages, dealers who regularly post price lists, stores with more than 15 products, refurbished-device sellers who need clear specifications, and dealers receiving repeated questions about price, stock, delivery, warranty, or payment.

A prospect is qualified when the owner can make decisions quickly, has a usable product list, can provide business contact details, and is willing to pay for domain/hosting/provider costs separately. Avoid spending a full day on a prospect who has no catalog, no business decision-maker, or no plan for receiving customer orders.

## 3. Discovery questions

Ask these questions before quoting a final scope. They also help the dealer describe the problems your system solves.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What products do you sell besides laptops? | Determines whether the catalog should include phones, accessories, monitors, parts, or other categories. |
| How many products should be live on day one? | Defines catalog-loading effort. |
| How do customers order today? | Reveals whether WhatsApp, calls, Instagram, or walk-ins need to remain part of the workflow. |
| Which cities do you deliver to? | Determines delivery zones, rates, and logistics configuration. |
| Do you accept card, transfer, USSD, or cash on delivery? | Determines payment modules and provider setup. |
| Do you have Paystack or Flutterwave already? | Avoids delaying the launch for merchant verification. |
| Do you have product photos and specifications? | Determines whether catalog setup can finish within 24 hours. |
| Do you offer warranty, returns, or testing? | Supplies the trust content customers need before purchase. |
| Who should receive new orders and customer questions? | Determines owner/staff access and notification setup. |
| Do you want reviews, email collection, coupons, chat, wishlist, or COD? | Determines the feature-toggle configuration. |
| Do you own a domain? | Determines whether DNS setup is part of the launch. |
| Who will update products after launch? | Determines training and whether ongoing maintenance should be quoted. |

## 4. Ready-to-use pitch

### Short WhatsApp or DM opener

> Hello [Name]. I noticed you sell [laptops/refurbished devices/accessories]. I build branded online stores for dealers that allow customers to browse real products, check specifications and prices, add to cart, choose delivery, and place orders. You also get a private admin dashboard where you can update products, stock, prices, coupons, orders, reviews, and sales information yourself. I can prepare a branded version for your business in 24 hours, excluding domain, hosting, and payment/logistics provider fees. Would you like to see a 7-minute demo?

### In-person or phone opener

> Most dealers already have products and customers. The problem is that sales information is scattered across WhatsApp messages, Instagram posts, and calls. I provide a proper online shop and a private control panel. Customers see a professional catalog, while you control the business from one place. The system is reusable, so I can brand it for your store without charging you for a completely new system.

### Discovery transition

> Before I show you the demo, I want to understand how you currently sell. How do customers check stock, confirm price, choose delivery, and pay today?

Listen for pain points such as repeated price questions, outdated WhatsApp posts, overselling, difficulty tracking orders, no customer database, lack of trust, and no clear way to measure online revenue. Repeat the dealer’s own words when presenting the solution.

### Seven-minute demo sequence

| Minute | Show | What to say |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–1 | Homepage and brand direction | “This is where we replace the demo identity with your store name, logo, colours, contact details, and product photography.” |
| 1–2 | Catalog and product detail | “Customers can browse categories and see price, condition, specifications, colours, sizes/options, weight, stock, and warranty information.” |
| 2–3 | Cart and checkout | “A customer can add multiple items, apply a coupon, choose a city, select payment, and submit the order without sending ten messages.” |
| 3–4 | Delivery and payment settings | “The store can start with manual delivery rates and later connect a logistics provider for live quotes, shipment creation, and tracking.” |
| 4–5 | Admin product manager | “You can add, edit, delete, reprice, restock, mark limited items, update categories, and manage product information from anywhere.” |
| 5–6 | Orders, inventory, coupons, reviews, and email leads | “This is the control room. You can see orders, manage stock, approve reviews, collect customer emails, and create promotions.” |
| 6–7 | Analytics and settings | “You can see revenue and product movement, then switch features on or off depending on what your business actually needs.” |

End the demo with a question rather than a long explanation:

> “If this was branded with your products and contact details today, which part would save you the most time: product browsing, checkout, stock control, or order management?”

### Closing question

> “Would you like the ₦50,000 starter launch or the ₦100,000 professional launch? To complete it within 24 hours, I need the business information, starter catalog, branding assets, and a deposit before I begin.”

## 5. Handling common objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “I already use WhatsApp.” | “Keep WhatsApp. The website gives customers a clear catalog and order path, while WhatsApp remains available for questions and follow-up. It reduces repeated messages instead of replacing your relationship with customers.” |
| “My customers may not use a website.” | “The website can be shared from WhatsApp, Instagram, Google, receipts, and business cards. It gives every product one link and makes the store easier to trust and revisit.” |
| “₦100,000 is expensive.” | “The price includes the storefront, admin system, catalog setup, deployment, and handover. Domain, hosting, and provider charges are separate because they belong to your business account. The ₦50,000 package is available with a smaller catalog and simpler customization.” |
| “Can I edit it myself?” | “Yes. You do not need to edit code for normal operations. The admin dashboard lets you update products, price, stock, coupons, orders, reviews, leads, and selected appearance settings.” |
| “Can customers pay online?” | “Yes, after your Paystack or Flutterwave merchant account and credentials are configured. The payment flow verifies transactions server-side and uses signed webhook events.” |
| “Can you calculate delivery automatically?” | “Yes, after connecting a supported logistics provider. The starter includes manual city rates immediately, and the provider adapter can use destination, weight, dimensions, and delivery settings for live quotes.” |
| “Can I send product emails?” | “Yes. The website can collect opted-in email leads. We can connect Mailchimp for campaigns or a transactional email provider for receipts and order notifications. Provider fees and setup are quoted separately.” |
| “Will you host my data with another dealer?” | “No. Every dealer receives a separate project, Supabase database, credentials, deployment, and domain. This is not a shared multi-tenant system.” |
| “What happens after launch?” | “You receive the repository, admin access, deployment details, and handover. I can quote monthly maintenance, catalog updates, marketing emails, and extra integrations separately.” |

## 6. Client information to collect before the 24-hour clock starts

Send the dealer one intake message. The 24-hour promise should start only after the required materials and deposit are received.

### Business and brand

Collect the legal or trading name, preferred store name, logo in PNG/SVG, brand colours, preferred font or visual style, tagline, support phone, WhatsApp number, support email, store address, opening hours, social links, and the preferred domain name.

### Catalog

Request a spreadsheet or structured list with one row per product. At minimum, collect product name, category, brand, SKU, price, comparison price, stock quantity, condition, description, image links/files, warranty, and delivery weight. For products that need them, also collect colours, sizes, variants/options, dimensions, tags, and specifications.

| Catalog field | Example |
|---|---|
| Name | Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 11 |
| Category | Laptops |
| SKU | LEN-X1C-11-I7 |
| Price | 1,480,000 |
| Sale/comparison price | 1,575,000 |
| Stock | 4 |
| Condition | Refurbished |
| Colours | Carbon black, Storm grey |
| Sizes/options | 16GB/512GB, 32GB/1TB |
| Weight | 1.12 kg |
| Specifications | Processor, memory, storage, display, battery, included items |
| Warranty/returns | 30-day warranty; dealer policy |
| Product images | Dealer-owned JPG/PNG files |

### Policies and operations

Collect delivery cities, delivery charges, delivery times, pickup rules, payment methods, cash-on-delivery rules, warranty policy, returns policy, exchange policy, privacy/contact wording, and who should receive new-order alerts. Never invent warranty, return, delivery, or payment claims on the dealer’s behalf.

### Provider access

Ask whether the dealer already has Paystack, Flutterwave, GIG Logistics, another logistics account, Mailchimp, Resend, SendGrid, or WhatsApp Business. Do not ask the dealer to send secrets through ordinary WhatsApp. Use a secure password manager or the hosting/provider dashboard, and keep secrets only in environment variables.

## 7. 24-hour deployment schedule

### Hour 0–1: Confirm scope and payment

Confirm the chosen package, number of products, design direction, feature toggles, delivery cities, payment method, and launch deadline. Collect the deposit and create a written record of what is included and excluded.

### Hour 1–2: Copy the standalone project

Create a new private repository or private project directory from the template. Do not reuse another dealer’s repository, Supabase project, database, domain, credentials, or customer data. Update the project name and deployment environment name.

### Hour 2–3: Create Supabase

Create a new Supabase project owned by the dealer or by the deployment account agreed in writing. In the Supabase SQL editor, run `supabase/migrations/202608270001_initial_dealer_commerce.sql`. Confirm that the tables, RLS policies, Auth profiles, product-images bucket, and storage policies were created.

Create the owner Auth user. Add the matching owner profile using the user UUID. Keep the service-role key server-side only. The Supabase RLS guidance should be followed for exposed tables and storage objects.[1]

### Hour 3–4: Configure environment variables

Copy `.env.example` to the deployment environment. Set `NODE_ENV=production`, `PORT`, `PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, `SUPABASE_AUTH_REQUIRED`, `SESSION_SECRET`, and the admin access values. Add payment, shipping, email, and WhatsApp credentials only when the client has approved and supplied them.

At minimum, generate a long random `SESSION_SECRET`. Do not commit `.env`, service-role keys, payment secrets, webhook secrets, or provider tokens.

### Hour 4–5: Seed and configure the business

Run `npm install` and `npm run supabase:seed`. Replace the demo business settings with the dealer’s name, tagline, contact information, colours, announcement, and feature toggles. If the dealer is using the current appearance form, save the settings through Admin → Settings.

Recommended first-launch toggles for a laptop dealer are reviews on, email collection on, variants on, delivery on, coupons on, chat on, COD only if the dealer has a clear policy, analytics on, payments on, and wishlist off until it is needed.

### Hour 5–8: Load and check the catalog

Load the agreed number of products. Verify every product name, price, sale price, SKU, stock count, condition, specification, warranty wording, image, colour, size/option, and weight. Check that products appear in the correct category and that sold-out or limited-stock states are accurate.

If bulk catalog loading is not included, load only the agreed starter number and quote the remainder separately. A smaller accurate catalog is better than a large catalog with incorrect prices or stock.

### Hour 8–10: Apply the UI redesign

Replace the demo identity with the dealer’s logo, colour palette, hero image, headline, support details, trust messages, category labels, and policy links. Keep the core checkout and admin behavior stable. Avoid changing payment or inventory logic while doing cosmetic redesign unless it is explicitly part of the scope.

### Hour 10–12: Configure delivery and payment

Start with the dealer’s approved delivery zones and manual rates. For live logistics, configure the provider adapter only after verifying the provider’s API requirements, origin address, destination format, weight/dimensions, account permissions, and tracking callbacks. GIG Logistics describes API-based delivery automation, tracking, payment-on-delivery, and reverse logistics capabilities; the exact account and endpoint setup still depends on the dealer’s provider access.[2]

For Paystack, configure the secret and webhook secret, set the webhook URL to `/api/webhooks/paystack`, and verify that the deployment uses HTTPS. Paystack supports Nigerian payment channels and asynchronous payment confirmation, so the server should verify the transaction and process webhook events instead of trusting only the browser return.[3]

### Hour 12–14: Configure email and customer communication

If the dealer wants marketing emails, connect Mailchimp using a server-side API key and audience ID, map the email-lead consent field, and test subscribe/unsubscribe behavior. Use a transactional provider such as Resend, Postmark, SendGrid, Mailgun, or SMTP for order receipts, payment confirmations, and delivery notifications. Keep marketing campaigns and transactional messages separate.

If chat is required, connect the approved WhatsApp Business or inbox provider. Until the credentials are live, keep the feature marked as demo or provider-ready rather than claiming that messages are connected.

### Hour 14–16: Deploy

Run:

```bash
npm install
npm run build
npm run start
```

The included Docker path can be used on a VPS or managed container host. For a managed Node host, configure the same environment variables in the host dashboard. Confirm that the API and built frontend are served from the correct public origin.

### Hour 16–18: Connect domain and HTTPS

Point the dealer’s domain DNS records to the selected host. Wait for DNS propagation as required by the provider. Confirm HTTPS, the public storefront, `/api/health`, admin login, and the payment webhook endpoint. Do not announce launch until the domain is serving the intended client copy rather than the template or another dealer.

### Hour 18–20: Run launch QA

Complete the checklist below using the deployed domain, not only localhost. Record screenshots or short screen recordings of the storefront, checkout, admin dashboard, product editing, and order flow for the handover.

### Hour 20–22: Dealer acceptance review

Walk the owner through the live storefront and admin dashboard. Ask the owner to verify the business name, prices, stock, contact details, policies, delivery rates, payment options, and order notifications. Let the owner approve the catalog and policy content in writing.

### Hour 22–24: Handover and launch

Create the final handover note with the domain, admin URL, Supabase project reference, hosting provider, enabled features, provider status, backup responsibility, support period, and outstanding paid add-ons. Change all temporary demo credentials. Confirm the owner can log in and update one product without your assistance.

## 9. Launch QA checklist

### Storefront

- The domain opens on HTTPS and shows the correct dealer name, logo, colours, contact details, and catalog.
- The home page, category pages, search, product details, cart, checkout, and order confirmation work on desktop and mobile widths.
- Product prices, sale prices, stock, categories, images, specifications, sizes, colours, options, weights, and warranty wording are correct.
- Disabled features are not presented to customers. For example, a dealer that does not want reviews or email collection should not show those blocks.
- Empty cart, no-result search, sold-out product, invalid coupon, invalid email, and unavailable API states show understandable messages.

### Checkout and orders

- A customer can place a test order using a test product or COD without using a real customer’s personal details.
- Stock decreases once for a successful order and does not decrease when the API rejects the request.
- The order contains customer name, phone, email where supplied, address, city, line items, total, payment state, and delivery information.
- Paystack test mode or approved live verification is completed before accepting real payments. The browser return alone is not treated as proof of payment.
- Delivery rates and estimated days match the dealer’s approved policy.

### Admin

- Owner login works and a wrong password is rejected.
- The owner can add, edit, delete, reprice, restock, and mark a product as limited.
- The owner can update categories, variants, sizes, colours, weight, descriptions, coupons, orders, reviews, email leads, appearance, and enabled features.
- The owner can see revenue, orders, low-stock products, top products, and relevant analytics.
- A second browser or unauthenticated request cannot access protected orders, coupons, leads, or management routes.

### Integrations and operations

- `/api/health` reports the intended production data mode.
- Secrets are present only in the hosting environment and are not in Git history or frontend bundles.
- Payment webhook signature verification and duplicate-event handling are tested.
- Email opt-in wording, unsubscribe path, and provider audience are verified before marketing sends.
- Logistics tracking and shipment status are verified if automation is enabled.
- A backup and recovery owner is identified for the Supabase project.

>

Please review product prices, stock, delivery charges, warranty/return policies, and payment settings before promoting the link. Your included support period is [period]. Additional catalog loading, marketing email setup, WhatsApp notifications, live logistics automation, and monthly maintenance are available separately.

## 11. What to charge separately

Charge separately for domain registration, hosting, payment-provider charges, logistics charges, email-platform charges, professional photography, bulk product entry, logo design, copywriting, WhatsApp messaging fees, custom SEO pages, paid advertising, staff accounts, monthly maintenance, and major custom functionality. These costs are not part of the reusable website engine and vary by business.

A clear maintenance offer might include monthly security and dependency updates, backups, small catalog changes, monitoring, and provider troubleshooting. Keep the recurring scope written and limited rather than promising unlimited support.
